# Chapter 2: I will now saw my [lovely] assistant in half

## Core Idea
Watch a real test before doing one. A DIY session has no magic and needs no special skills. Participants vary in how much they surface, but on average each one teaches you a lot. *(This is a thin chapter that exists to make you watch Krug's demo video.)*

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Watch with a job**: while watching any test (the demo, or later your own), keep in mind that at the end you'll **list the three usability problems you'd most want to fix** if it were your site. This is the same "top three" discipline observers use (ch09) and the input to the debrief (ch10).

## Key Concepts
- **Demo test**: Krug's recorded ~15-minute abbreviated session: a Home page tour plus one task. It's available on the book's companion site.
- **Real session length**: typically about an hour, with more or longer tasks. Tests can range from five minutes to a full day.

## Mental Models
- **"Is that all there is?" Yes.** The shape never changes: welcome, a few questions, the Home page tour, tasks with think-aloud, probing, wrap-up. The ch08 timeline is this demo at full length.

## Anti-patterns
- **Skipping the demo because it looks trivial.** Krug gave it a whole chapter because watching one is what convinces you that you can run one.

## Key Takeaways
1. Watch a complete test (any recorded one) before facilitating your first.
2. While watching, force yourself to name the top three problems.
3. Expect variation between participants, but some learning from each one.
