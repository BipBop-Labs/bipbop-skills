# Chapter 7: Some boring checklists

## Core Idea
A test day has many time-bound details. Checklists get them out of your head so you can focus on the participant. The one item everyone forgets is **starting the screen recorder**.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The countdown**:
  - **Three weeks before**: decide what you'll test (site, wireframes, prototype); list the tasks; decide who to test with; advertise for participants; book the test room for the whole morning (internet, desk, two chairs, speakerphone) plus a waiting spot nearby; book the observation room (internet, enough chairs, speakerphone, projector or large screen); book a room for the debrief lunch.
  - **Two weeks before**: get feedback on the task list from the team and stakeholders; arrange incentives; start screening and scheduling; send a "save the date" to the team and stakeholders.
  - **One week before**: email participants directions, parking, the room, a day-of contact, and the NDA if you use one; line up a **standby participant**; on your first round, install and test the recording and screen-sharing software.
  - **One or two days before**: reconfirm participants by phone; remind observers; finish the scenarios; **pilot them**; gather credentials and sample data (test accounts, dummy card numbers); print participant handouts (consent form, one scenario per sheet, spare NDAs) and observer handouts (observer instructions, scenario list, test script); recruit a **Designated Greeter** and a **Hall Monitor** (give them the Hall Monitor's Guide); prepare incentives; check the mic, speakers, and cables; order snacks; make sure nobody double-booked your rooms.
  - **Test day, before the first session**: order the debrief lunch; put handouts in the observation room; confirm the thing under test is installed or reachable and working; **record a short clip with audio and play it back**; test screen sharing with the observation room; disable interruptions (email, chat, reminders, scheduled scans); bookmark the pages you'll need; have the phone numbers you need; check both speakerphones.
  - **Before each session**: clear browser history; open a neutral page.
  - **While the participant signs the consent form**: **start the screen recorder!**
  - **After each session**: **stop the recorder, save the recording**; end screen sharing; jot notes before the next session; after the last session, copy the recordings off the test machine.

## Key Concepts
- **Designated Greeter**: welcomes participants, seats them, and escorts them in.
- **Hall Monitor**: runs the observation room (see ch09).
- **Neutral page**: a start page (e.g., a search engine's home) that gives no hints about the product.

## Mental Models
- **Checklists buy attention.** The facilitator's working memory belongs to the participant, not the logistics.
- **Adapt the lead times** to your organization, since approval for incentive money or catering can take longer.

## Anti-patterns
- Trusting memory for the recorder. Krug guarantees you'll forget it once, which is why the reminder is also written into the script.
- Setting up on test day with no time buffer.
- Leaving browser history in place, where visited-link colors give clues.

## Key Takeaways
1. Work backward from test day in four steps: three weeks, two weeks, one week, one or two days.
2. Pilot scenarios and test the recording rig *before* the day, then again on the day.
3. Start the recorder while the consent form is being signed. Stop and save it after every session.
4. Reset the machine between sessions.
5. Delegate greeting and observation-room duty.
