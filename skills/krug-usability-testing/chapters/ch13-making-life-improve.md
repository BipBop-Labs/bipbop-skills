# Chapter 13: Making sure life actually improves

## Core Idea
Agreed-upon fixes often never ship. Surviving that takes durable commitment from management, the team, and stakeholders, and the most reliable way to build it is **demonstration, not argument**: let watching tests turn people into believers.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Why things don't get fixed** (Krug's clients often agreed enthusiastically, and then nothing changed for months or years):
  - A change of management, of direction, or both.
  - **Putting things off**: when a fix gets harder than expected, "that'll have to wait for the redesign" (translation: the check is in the mail).
  - Insufficient buy-in from the right people.
  - **Sabotage**: people who felt voiceless in choosing fixes drag their feet.
  - **Eyes bigger than your stomach**: too many fixes committed.
  - **Deep roots**: the problem is a symptom of an unresolved conflict about the product's purpose or the company's mission.
  - Above all, **life intervenes**: no time, resources, or commitment to follow through.
- **Selling usability to management, three options**:
  1. **Persuasion**: learn management's goals and language and present regularly. A good idea.
  2. **ROI arguments**: convincing, especially for intranets where savings are quantifiable (e.g., minutes saved per employee × salary × headcount), but slow and costly to produce. Usability is also "last hired, first fired" when money is tight, and it loses to "ship now" because managers assume people will muddle through.
  3. **Demonstration (Krug's preference)**: "Don't try to make converts: let the seeing-is-believing effect of watching usability testing make converts for you." It's easier, longer lasting, and **recession-proof**. You want **fanatics** who can't imagine not investing in user experience, because buy-in evaporates in lean times.

## Key Concepts
- **Big Honkin' Report**: the polished, screenshot-rich report that everyone praised and nobody acted on.
- **Conversion experience**: the moment an observer becomes a believer by watching.
- **Fanatic**: someone for whom good UX is non-negotiable, not merely profitable.

## Mental Models
- **Reports don't cause change; people do.** Krug's best reports, with top-ten priorities and easy fixes that executives agreed to, frequently produced nothing.
- **Companies that need ROI proof probably won't do great work anyway.** Great work requires passion to do it right.
- **Participation prevents sabotage.** Everyone who watches gets a voice in the debrief, so they own the fixes.

## Anti-patterns
- Relying on a report or presentation to drive fixes.
- Overcommitting in the debrief.
- Deferring to future redesigns.
- Excluding stakeholders from choosing what to fix.
- Leaning on ROI decks as the main persuasion strategy.

## Key Takeaways
1. Expect fixes to stall, and design the process against it: a small scope, short commitments, a monthly cadence.
2. Give everyone who watched a voice in what gets fixed.
3. Commit only to what fits this month's capacity.
4. Treat fixes that reveal deep strategic conflicts as a separate conversation, but still mitigate the symptom.
5. Convert management by getting them into the observation room, not by arguing.
