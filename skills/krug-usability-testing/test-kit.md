# Test Kit: templates to adapt

These are condensed templates based on the book's method. Adapt the wording to your product and language, then **say the facilitator script the same way every session**. Replace every `[placeholder]`.

## 1. Participant invitation
> We're testing a [website / app] on the morning of [date] and are looking for a few people to help. It takes about an hour at [location / online]. We're especially looking for people who [qualifying behavior, e.g., "have paid a bill online in the last month"]. If you're interested and free that day, email [contact email] with your name, a phone number, and a good time to reach you.

Ask for email replies, not calls.

## 2. Screening call checklist
- [ ] Available on test day. Book a slot.
- [ ] Meets the qualifications (ask neutrally, since some people stretch the truth).
- [ ] Expectations set: about an hour, using a [site / app], screen and voice recorded, face not recorded.
- [ ] Compensation explained.
- [ ] Sounds articulate and comfortable talking aloud?
- [ ] Remote only: quick screen-sharing check.

## 3. Scenario template
> You are [role / situation]. You need to [goal in the user's own words]. [Only the context they need.] [Test credentials or data, if needed.]

Checks: no words copied from on-screen labels; no unnecessary detail; one scenario per sheet, large type, **unnumbered**; piloted with someone.

## 4. Facilitator script (condensed; read it, don't improvise)
**Welcome (~4 min)**
- Hi [name], I'm [name] and I'll be walking you through this session. I'll read some information so I cover everything.
- We're asking people to try a [site / app] we're working on, to see whether it works as intended. It'll take about an hour.
- **We're testing the [site / app], not you.** You can't do anything wrong here.
- Please **think out loud** as much as you can: what you're looking at, what you're trying to do, what you're thinking.
- You won't hurt our feelings. We need your honest reactions.
- If you have questions, ask, but I may not answer until the end, because we want to see how people do on their own. Tell me if you need a break.
- With your permission we'll record the screen and our voices, only for the people working on this project. A few team members are watching the screen from another room.
- Please sign this recording permission form. → **Start the recorder while they sign.**
- Any questions so far?

**Questions (~2 min)**
- What do you do for a living?
- Roughly how many hours a week do you spend online, at work and at home?
- What kinds of sites or apps do you use most? Any favorites?
(Follow up on at least one answer, and really listen.)

**Home page / first-screen tour (~3 min)**
- Look at this page and tell me what you make of it: what strikes you, whose it is, what you can do here, and what it's for. You can scroll, but please don't click anything yet.

**Tasks (~35 min)**
- Now I'll ask you to do some specific tasks. I'll read each one aloud and give you a copy. Please don't use search. And again, please think out loud.
- → Hand over each scenario and read it aloud. Continue until the task is done, the participant is too frustrated, or it stops being useful.

**Probing (~5 min)**
- Thanks, that was very helpful. Excuse me a minute while I check whether the team has any questions. → Call the observation room, then ask their questions and your deferred whys.

**Wrap-up (~5 min)**
- Do you have any questions for me now that we're done? → Give the incentive, **stop and save the recording**, thank them, walk them out.

## 5. Neutral responses
| When | Say |
|---|---|
| Unsure what they're thinking | "What are you thinking?" / "What are you looking at?" / "What are you doing now?" |
| They seem surprised | "Is that what you expected to happen?" |
| They fish for a clue | "What would you do if you were at home?" → "Then go ahead and try that." / "I'd like you to do whatever you'd normally do." |
| A comment with an unclear trigger | "Was there something in particular that made you think that?" |
| They worry they're doing badly | "No, this is very helpful." / "This is exactly what we need." |
| They ask how something works | "What do you think?" / "How do you think it would work?" / "I'll gladly answer that when we're done." |
| They wander off the task | "What are you trying to do now?" |
| Moving on | "That's great, very helpful. I want to move **us** along, since **we**'ve got more to do." |

## 6. Recording consent form
> Thank you for taking part in our usability research. We will record this session so that [organization] staff can see how the [site / app] works for people.
> I understand my session will be recorded, and I give [organization] permission to use the recording only to improve the designs being tested.
> Signature ____  Printed name ____  Date ____

## 7. Observer instructions (one page)
- Sessions last about 50 minutes, with a 10-minute break between them.
- **Take notes**, especially where the participant got confused or couldn't finish.
- **After each session, write the three most serious problems you saw** (use the sheet below).
- Write down questions for the participant. We'll collect them before probing.
- **Come to the debrief** at [time] in [room]. Lunch is provided. Only people who watched at least one session take part.
- Stay as long as you can, and come and go quietly.
- Keep conversation to what you're watching. Take calls outside.

**Top three problems**: Participant 1: 1. __ 2. __ 3. __ · Participant 2: 1. __ 2. __ 3. __ · Participant 3: 1. __ 2. __ 3. __

## 8. Debrief agenda (~1 hour)
Write on the pad first: *Stick to what you observed · Focus on the most serious problems · Goal: a list we'll fix in the next month.*
1. Explain: pick the top ten, rank them, commit to fixes for the month.
2. Everyone picks their top three.
3. Go around and read them out ("I had that too").
4. Write them all on the pad.
5. Facilitator proposes the top ten and takes objections.
6. Rewrite the top ten ranked, worst first.
7. For each one, in order and with no skipping: what's the least we can do this month?
8. Stop when this month's capacity is committed.

## 9. Summary email (≤2 min read)
> **Usability testing, [month]**
> - Tested: [what]
> - Tasks: [list]
> - We'll fix before the next round: 1. [problem → planned tweak] 2. […] 3. […]
> - Recordings: [where]. Next round: [date].
