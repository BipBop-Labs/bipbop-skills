# Krug Usability Testing Glossary

**A/B test** — Splitting live traffic between the original and a tweaked page to measure which gets more people to the target. One way to verify a fix. (Ch 11)

**Acknowledgment token** — "Uh huh", "OK", "mm hmm". It signals *understood*, not *agreed*. (Ch 8)

**Big Bang Theory of Web Usability** — The first few seconds on a site set working assumptions that shape everything after. Wrong ones snowball. (Ch 12)

**Big Honkin' Test** — The formal, off-site, expensive, late, rarely done alternative to DIY testing. (Ch 3)

**Big Honkin' Report** — The long, polished findings report that rarely leads to fixes. (Ch 13)

**Comp** — A visual treatment of a page or template, tested by walkthrough and narrative. (Ch 4)

**Corner case** — A severe problem that affects few users. One of the hard severity calls. (Ch 10)

**Debriefing** — A same-day meeting, over lunch, of people who observed. It produces a ranked top ten and a fix commitment. (Ch 10)

**Designated Greeter** — The person who welcomes, seats, and escorts participants. (Ch 7)

**Discount usability testing** — Nielsen's term for simple, informal, small-sample testing. The book's subject. (Ch 1)

**Do-it-yourself (DIY) testing** — Qualitative, small-sample testing done by the team itself to improve, not prove. (Ch 1)

**Domain knowledge** — Subject-matter expertise. It matters less for usability problems than teams assume. (Ch 5)

**Facilitator** — The person in the room with the participant, acting as tour guide and therapist. (Ch 8)

**Failure to shout** — Important things too subtle to notice. (Ch 12)

**Filler task** — A spare task for fast participants, often done on a competitor's site. (Ch 6)

**Getting off on the wrong foot** — Starting with a misapprehension and confidently heading the wrong way. (Ch 12)

**Grade on a curve** — Discounting failures caused only by a participant's missing jargon or domain knowledge. (Ch 5)

**Hall Monitor** — The person who runs the observation room for the facilitator. (Ch 9)

**Hallway test** — An informal check with almost anyone on a single task, often to verify a tweak. (Ch 11)

**Heir and a spare** — Always having a standby participant on call. (Ch 5)

**Home page tour** — "Tell me what you make of this page", no clicking, about 3 minutes. It tests orientation. (Ch 8)

**Kitchen-sink syndrome** — A Home page overloaded by stakeholder additions. (Ch 12)

**Krug's First Law of Usability Testing** — Testing with one user is 100% better than testing with none. (Ch 5)

**Least you can do** — The smallest, simplest change likely to prevent the *observed* problem. (Ch 11)

**Louder** — A stronger version of the same tweak. (Ch 11)

**Maxim** — One of Krug's six rules of thumb. (Ch 16)

**Morning a month** — The baseline cadence: three tests plus a debrief, monthly. (Ch 3)

**Napkin test** — A sub-5-minute concept test: "What do you think this is supposed to be?" (Ch 4)

**Observer** — A team member watching remotely from a separate room who lists the top three problems per session. (Ch 9)

**Pilot test** — A ~15-minute dry run of the scenarios to catch ambiguity. (Ch 6)

**Probing** — Post-task questions about noticing and choices, plus observers' questions. (Ch 8)

**Qualitative testing** — Testing for insight, with a flexible protocol and small samples. (Ch 1)

**Quantitative testing** — Testing to prove, with a fixed protocol, measurement, and a significant sample. (Ch 1)

**Remote testing (moderated)** — The same process over screen sharing and voice. (Ch 14)

**Remote testing (unmoderated)** — A service-recruited participant does short tasks alone while thinking aloud, with no probing. (Ch 14)

**Ringer** — A deliberately non-target participant, one per round. (Ch 5)

**Scenario** — A task wrapped in context and needed data, written without on-screen words. (Ch 6)

**Screener** — The phone call that checks availability, qualifications, and articulateness. (Ch 5)

**Severity** — How many people it affects × how badly. (Ch 10)

**SquirmCam** — Recording the participant's face. Krug considers it an unnecessary distraction. (Ch 8)

**Stay neutral** — Give no clues, answer questions with questions, share no opinions, keep a poker face. (Ch 8)

**Task** — A user goal to attempt. (Ch 6)

**Test participant** — Not "subject"; the product is what's being tested. (Ch 1)

**Think-aloud protocol** — The participant narrates what they look at, attempt, and think. The "secret sauce". (Ch 8)

**Top ten** — The debrief's ranked list of the most serious observed problems. (Ch 10)

**Tweak** — A slight change to size, position, appearance, or wording, iterated. (Ch 11)

**Ubiquitous nuisance** — A minor problem that affects many users. One of the hard severity calls. (Ch 10)

**Wireframe** — A page schematic, tested with navigation tasks. (Ch 4)
